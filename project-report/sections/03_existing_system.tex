\subsection*{3.1 Introduction}
The current online gambling and crypto casino sector is overwhelmingly dominated by centralized web platforms such as Stake.com, Roobet, and traditional online casinos licensed in offshore jurisdictions. These systems allow users to create accounts, deposit cryptocurrencies or fiat currencies into hot wallets managed by the casino company, and wager across various digital casino games. However, they lack a decentralized, trustless architecture, forcing players to rely on blind faith in the operator's integrity.

Most existing solutions claim to provide fair odds, but the algorithms generating the random numbers remain proprietary and hosted on private servers. As a result, these systems fail to provide transparent, tamper-proof guarantees of mathematical fairness. This architectural centralization fundamentally weakens user trust and leaves players exposed to severe financial and data security risks.

\subsection*{3.2 Features of the Existing System}
The existing centralized online casinos provide standard functionalities for user engagement and wagering:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{User Registration and Accounts:} Platforms require players to register using an email address, password, or third-party OAuth account (Google, Facebook).
    \item \textbf{Centralized Custodial Banking:} Players deposit cryptocurrency (Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT) into custodial deposit addresses generated by the casino. Balances are held directly by the company rather than in smart contract escrow.
    \item \textbf{Proprietary Game Loops:} Games such as dice, roulette, slots, and crash are rendered using standard 2D web canvases or pre-rendered sprite animations driven by private server-side random number generators.
    \item \textbf{Gamification and Retention Features:} Existing systems incorporate VIP tier ladders, daily bonus wheels, rakeback rewards, and live trollbox chat rooms to maximize user engagement.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{3.3 Challenges in the Existing System}
One of the major challenges in the existing system is the total lack of cryptographic verifiability. Because users cannot independently audit the private server seed or the internal RNG before placing their wagers, operators can manipulate roll results to favor the house during high-stakes rounds without leaving an auditable trail.

Another critical challenge is the complete absence of fund custody. When players transfer funds to centralized casinos, they surrender legal and cryptographic ownership of their assets. Numerous instances of platform insolvencies, frozen withdrawals, and sudden account closures demonstrate that centralized custody poses unacceptable counterparty risk.

Furthermore, existing systems subject users to invasive Know Your Customer (KYC) identity verification procedures. Players are routinely forced to upload government passports, national IDs, and proof of address before being permitted to withdraw winnings. This exposes user identities to severe data breach risks and privacy violations.

\subsection*{3.4 Workflow of the Existing System}
The typical workflow of existing centralized gambling systems proceeds through the following sequence:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item The user visits the platform and completes an account registration form providing email and password credentials.
    \item The platform creates a custodial wallet address for the user and requests a cryptocurrency deposit.
    \item Once the blockchain transaction confirms, the platform's private database increments the user's virtual balance.
    \item The user selects a game and clicks ``Bet''. A request is transmitted to the casino's private server.
    \item The server executes a closed-source RNG function, determines whether the user won or lost, updates the database balance, and returns the animation response to the frontend.
    \item When the user attempts to withdraw their balance, the request is routed to a human or automated compliance team. The platform may demand identity verification documents (KYC) and holds discretion over whether to approve or cancel the payout.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{3.5 Limitations of the Existing System}
The existing system suffers from several structural limitations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Counterparty and Insolvency Risk:} Player assets are commingled with the company's operating capital, creating high risk of total loss if the platform becomes insolvent.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Mathematical Proof:} No mathematical proof of fairness is available in real time; audit certifications are static, periodic, and issued by paid third-party testing agencies.
    \item \textbf{Arbitrary Account Confiscation:} Terms of service grant operators unilateral authority to void bets, freeze balances, or deny payouts at their sole discretion.
    \item \textbf{Primitive 2D Graphics:} Most crypto gaming sites rely on simplistic 2D charts and low-frame-rate canvas animations, lacking modern hardware acceleration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{3.6 Need for a New System}
The challenges and limitations of the existing centralized ecosystem highlight the critical need for a modern, blockchain-anchored gaming platform. A system that integrates cryptographic provable fairness with non-custodial smart contracts can eliminate counterparty risk, prevent game tampering, and guarantee self-custodial ownership of assets.

Such a platform must enable gas-free, zero-KYC wallet authentication, deliver 60 FPS 3D WebGL graphics, and support instant, cryptographically signed withdrawals. CypherRoll is engineered from the ground up to address these needs by introducing a hybrid Web3 architecture for online gaming.
